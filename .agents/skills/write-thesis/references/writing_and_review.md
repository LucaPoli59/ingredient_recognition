# Argument, drafting and review

Read this for an outline, a new section, language revision or scientific review. The [editorial requirements](../../../../docs/research/topics/thesis_writing/author_voice.md) are authoritative; these are implementation choices for applying them.

## Build the argument

Locate the existing manuscript and its notes before creating parallel files. For the first manuscript, propose a small `thesis/` workspace with chapter Markdown, one bibliography, figures and separate evidence notes; do not create empty chapter scaffolds simply to demonstrate setup. Keep durable project facts in their existing documentation owners.

Map each planned chapter to the question it answers, the evidence available and the conclusion it can support. The earlier [chapter proposal](../../../../docs/research/topics/thesis_writing/tools_and_workflow.md#proposed-structure-for-this-project) is a starting point, not an adopted table of contents. Stable background and methods may precede final results. Distinguish planned experiments from performed ones in tense and wording.

Before explaining a consequential choice, inspect its recorded rationale and relevant alternatives. If the rationale is absent, ask the author a focused question while continuing independent work; do not reverse-engineer an intention from a favorable outcome. Explain trade-offs where they matter, rather than giving every implementation detail a justification paragraph.

## Draft or revise at the requested scale

Start with a small evidence-backed argument, then write connected English prose. Organize related work by the questions and differences that motivate this project, not one paper per paragraph. Explain only the concepts, notation and examples needed for the next inference. Equations need defined quantities and a reason for appearing.

For an editorial-only request, preserve scientific meaning and flag a questionable claim separately; do not silently change the experiment or add a stronger conclusion. For substantive revision, verify the replacement claim before strengthening it. Keep unresolved sources and result-dependent material in working notes.

Use a terminology/notation note when several chapters need consistency: ingredient labels versus visible ingredients, multi-label versus multiclass, AP/mAP aggregation, logits/probabilities, threshold policy, model variant and dataset generation. Define abbreviations at first use and preserve the project's exact model names. Avoid introducing synonyms for technical terms just to vary the prose.

## Review for decisions, not a score

Scientific review identifies an exact manuscript location, evidence, why it matters and a bounded remedy. Distinguish missing reporting from a demonstrated contradiction or a design limitation. Do not invent defects to populate a review template, pretend this is independent peer review, or demand new experiments automatically.

Compare abstract, results, discussion and conclusion claims for scope and numerical consistency. Separate the observation from its possible explanation. State negative or inconclusive findings directly. Future work should address a concrete limitation or unanswered question, without implying that unrun experiments succeeded.

In the editorial pass, read more than one section together. Check for repeated paragraph shapes, obligatory end summaries, interchangeable introductions and phrases that could describe any machine-learning project. Remove a sentence when it contributes no evidence, reasoning, necessary definition or useful connection. Use lists and subheadings only when they improve navigation or comparison. Do not replace repetition with ornate wording, deliberate mistakes or arbitrary sentence variation.

Accept the section only when its claims are supportable and its argument is clear at the needed depth. The author should be able to explain the central claim, evidence and strongest limitation orally. Report actual missing inputs without turning every preference into a blocking approval.
